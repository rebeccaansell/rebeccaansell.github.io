I'm a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at Georgetown University. My research focuses on fine-tuning large language models, perceived humanness of AI-generated content, and computational social science applications.

## Current Research

I lead two major research initiatives:

**Humanness Project**: A suite of papers examining how humans perceive AI-generated content. My work includes quantifying human judgments of AI humanness, developing predictive models using the Perceived Humanness Index (PHI), and identifying stylometric signatures across different chatbot families.

**Public Debt is Public**: Fine-tuning large language models to automatically classify sovereign debt contract provisions for a platform providing transparent, searchable debt documentation to policymakers and civil society worldwide.

## Background

I completed my M.S. in Computer Science at Georgetown University in 2025, where I developed the Perceived Humanness Indicator (PHI) and conducted comparative analysis of fine-tuning methods. I received my B.S. in Computer Science from Long Island University with Magna Cum Laude honors and a 3.89 GPA.

Currently, I serve as a Data Science Researcher at the Massive Data Institute, where I conduct interdisciplinary research on AI-generated content detection and misinformation. I'm passionate about applying computational methods to understand social and political phenomena in the age of AI.
